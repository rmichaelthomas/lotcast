    Why it's fair

    Your request is sealed before chance arrives.

    1. You ask

      Your question stays with you. Only its fingerprint goes out.

    2. It's written down

      Into a public log, time-stamped by outside services.

    3. Chance arrives

      Fresh random numbers from public sources, made after you asked.

    4. Fixed rules

      Published rules turn the numbers into your draw. No one chooses.

    5. A sealed receipt

      This page checks it. So can anyone else.

    What does the check look at?

    Six steps, in order. The first one that fails is named.

    1. Entry and promise. Your request is the one that went into the log, and the operator signed a promise to answer it in a named time slot.
    2. Log. The log's signed checkpoint contains that entry.
    3. Timestamps. Independent time-stamp authorities stamped that checkpoint, so it existed before the random numbers did.
    4. Sources. Each public randomness beacon pulse in the receipt is genuine, and is the first one after the deadline.
    5. Outcome. The result follows from the request, the checkpoint and those numbers by a fixed method anyone can re-run.
    6. Question. If you asked a question, the receipt holds only a commitment to it. This box can't check it, because the question stays on your device; the Save my question file lets lotcast verify do it.

    This page checks the offline half only. It cannot ask a beacon whether it has since published a pulse for your draw, so a receipt that depends on an idle source is marked as not fully checked.
